**What it does.** Redline is a Snap Spectacles Lens that fills your real floor with a life-size ghost crowd at a density you choose. You fit a zone to your space with four corner handles (up to 10 × 10 m), then drag one slider on a see-through clipboard from 0 to 7 people per square metre. The zone fills with exactly that many standing figures per square metre, 155 to 185 cm tall and in five shades so they read as separate people. The floor turns green, amber, then red, and at 5 per m² a low, calm tone plays once. You can walk into the middle, where bodies fade out of a 0.6 m circle around you, and look down at one highlighted square metre to count the pairs of feet packed into it. A Test your eye mode hides the number and the colours, asks for your guess, then reveals the truth with a small top-down map: "You guessed 4, it was 6".

**Who it's for.** Student organizers and volunteer stewards who run gigs, festivals, graduations and other events, and have to judge by eye when a space is getting dangerous. Crowd-safety guidance puts the danger point for standing crowds at about 5 people per square metre, but from eye level 4 and 6 per m² look almost the same, and a number on a safety plan means little until you've stood inside it. Redline lets you stand inside it safely, in your own empty venue, before the doors open. It's a training tool, not a certified safety system.

**What I learned.** AR on glasses has its own rules. The display adds light, so dark means invisible: my first translucent white figures merged into a haze until I made them solid blue-grey with a brighter head and shoulders. Specs' view is narrow, so the clipboard moved from my waist up to just below my line of sight, and became see-through so it doesn't hide the crowd. Planning one spatial moment in a design brief first kept the build focused and testable, and nine LEAF tests now pass in the preview. But automated tests that press buttons directly can pass while real clicks fail, and a small checked-in maths test caught a crowd-counting bug the Lens tests missed. Feel mattered as much as logic: a calm tone instead of an alarm made the redline something you sense rather than read.
